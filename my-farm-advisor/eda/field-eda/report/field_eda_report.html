Data & method. Boundaries are grower-drawn polygons imported by the data pipeline (areas in EPSG:5070). Weather is NASA POWER daily data sampled at each field centroid; GDD use the 86/50 °F corn method over Apr 1–Sep 30. Crops are the majority USDA CDL class inside each boundary (30 m), computed with the pipeline's cdl-cropland zonal routine. Pivot status is grower-supplied.

Dataset scope and authorized substitution. This report analyses 30 field boundaries in Lee Co., AR; Pemiscot Co., MO; and Coahoma Co., MS. The brief names an Illinois/Iowa/Nebraska sample; this run uses the author's own field boundaries in three Mid-South states under instructor authorization, and runs the same pipeline steps on them — boundary import, SSURGO soil, DEM terrain with state lidar, NASA POWER weather, CDL 2019–2025, and multi-year satellite imagery. The layers and the comparison levels are unchanged; only the geography and crop mix differ. Corn is 14%–20% of acreage at every grower and 4 grower-confirmed center pivots are included, so the corn/soy and pivot-irrigation context the brief asks for is represented alongside Delta cotton and peanut rotations.

1 · Field boundaries

Levels used: field (figures 1–2) and across-grower (figure 3). Boundaries have no time dimension, so field-year does not apply to this category.

  • Median field size runs from 37 ac (MO) to 89 ac (AR); across all 30 fields it spans 9.9–226.7 ac. Kruskal–Wallis p = 0.187, so with 10 fields per grower the size difference is not statistically clear.
  • MO fields are the most regular (median rectangularity 0.92) and AR the least (0.68; p = 0.003). Small fields carry the most boundary per acre, i.e. the most headland and turn-row.
  • Center pivots (grower-confirmed): AR 2 (26% of acres), MO 1 (30% of acres), MS 1 (9% of acres).

2 · Weather

Levels used: field-year (figure 5 — a field's season-to-season weather is its grower's curve, because a farm shares one or two NASA POWER cells) and across-grower (figures 4 and 6). Field-to-field weather inside a farm is not separable at this resolution.

  • Apr–Sep GDD averages 4,201 °F (MO, 36.02°N) to 4,494 °F (MS, 34.36°N): a 293 °F-day gap between farms 195 km apart.
  • Season rainfall averages 24.5 in (MO) to 26.6 in (MS); days ≥ 95 °F average 11 (MO) to 38 (MS). Rainfall swings 10.6 in between a grower's wettest and driest season, more than the 2.1 in gap between growers.
  • Within each farm, fields differ by at most 0 GDD in any season: NASA POWER meteorology is a 0.5° × 0.625° grid, so a farm's fields share one or two cells. Weather here compares growers, not fields within a farm.

3 · Cropland (CDL)

Levels used: field-year within a field (figure 7), field-year across fields (figure 8) and across-grower (figure 9).

  • Acre-weighted crop mix, all years: AR mostly Cotton (48%); MO mostly Soybeans (38%); MS mostly Cotton (47%).
  • The crop changes from the previous year on 51% of acres per year at MS versus 74% at AR.
  • The majority crop covers a median 95% of a field-year. Lowest: LC 04 (76%), CH 01 (83%), HO 09 (89%). Across fields purity does not track field size (Spearman ρ = 0.08, p = 0.684), so mixed labels come from split or double-cropped fields rather than from small fields alone.

Limits

  • 10 fields per grower: tests are indicative, and acreage differs by grower (AR 911 ac, MO 520 ac, MS 709 ac).
  • NASA POWER cannot resolve field-to-field weather inside a farm; on-farm stations would be needed for that.
  • CDL is a classification, not a planting record: edge pixels, double crops and misclassification all lower the majority share.
  • Soil is out of scope by design. Soil analysis is not required for this assignment and no soil variable is used or tested here. SSURGO and SoilGrids outputs do exist for these fields from the Assignment 1 pipeline, but they are deliberately left out of this EDA.
